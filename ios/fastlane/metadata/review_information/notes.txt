Puki is a game for toddlers (ages 2 to 4) in Hebrew and English. No account or login is needed. The first screen (for a parent) starts in Hebrew: tap "English" at the top to switch everything, the parent check included, to English.

Parent gate: the settings (gear, top corner of the map) open after a two-second press and hold, then tapping the number written in words. Mail links, the privacy policy link, the brain download and every other grown-up option are behind it.

Optional on-device AI: in the settings, "Puki on this device" downloads small ONNX models once (about 0.5 GB, from Hugging Face, plus one small voice file from GitHub). They run entirely on the device; nothing the child says or does is sent anywhere. Puki's lines stay inside the game: they are spoken by the parrot, never shown to or shared with anyone else, and nobody can message the child. Before the model answers, what the child said passes word lists and a safety check: hurt, danger, secrets and big questions get a fixed, gentle reply that points to a parent, and every generated line is filtered (no promises, no "best friend"); a line that fails is replaced by a recorded one. Without the download, the parrot uses recorded lines and every game works. The microphone is used only when the child holds the parrot to talk or answers a question out loud; recordings are never stored.

The app collects no data and contains no ads, analytics or third-party SDKs. It is open source (MIT): https://github.com/NaimDor/puki-iOS
